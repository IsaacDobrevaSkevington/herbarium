Session 2: Culinary Creations with Summer Herbs

60 minutes

Introduction

Summer is the season when many herbs are at their peak—vibrant, aromatic, and full of life. It’s the perfect time to bring their fresh flavors and gentle support into everyday meals and beverages. These herbs do more than enhance taste; many offer nourishing minerals, support digestion, and add a naturally cooling quality that’s especially welcome in warm weather. Whether you’re tossing together a crisp salad, infusing a drink, or finishing a dish with a bright herbal garnish, summer herbs can turn simple food into something special.

This lesson is all about exploring that abundance. You’ll find refreshing, easy-to-make recipes that invite you to experiment, savor the season, and share something delicious with the people you love.

Refreshing Summer Drinks

Cool off with these vibrant, herb-infused drinks—perfect for warm weather and slow summer moments. Each recipe offers a simple, delicious way to weave herbs into creative and delicious teas and drinks we can enjoy daily during the season for hydration, cooling, relaxation, and fun.

Summer Bloom Sun Tea

Sun tea is a simple, refreshing way to brew tea using the warmth of the sun. This gentle steeping method is perfect for hot summer days and is a wonderful way to enjoy nature’s slow brewing process. Follow these easy steps to create your own flavorful and hydrating sun tea while keeping it safe and delicious!

Ingredients

2 tablespoon hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa) calyx
1 tablespoon chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) flower 
½ cinnamon (Cinnamomum spp.) stick, crushed
Filtered water to fill jar

Directions

  • Place all ingredients into a sanitized 1 quart glass jar.
  • Fill your jar with filtered water.
  • Place your jar in a sunny spot, covered by a lid or piece of cheesecloth and rubberband.
  • Let your sun tea infuse in the sunshine for about 3 hours.
  • Strain out the herbs and enjoy! Sweeten with a little honey, if desired. 

Safety Tips

While sun tea is a delightful tradition, it’s essential to be aware of potential bacterial growth due to the lukewarm temperatures during brewing. To minimize risks, ensure your container is thoroughly sanitized before use.​ After brewing, refrigerate the tea promptly, and consume it within 24 hours.​

Spearmint and Lemon Balm Summer Slushie

Beat the heat with a refreshing herbal slushie!  It’s the perfect way to enjoy the season’s herbal abundance in a frosty, feel-good drink that’s as beautiful as it is delicious.

Ingredients

3 lemon slices
Handful of spearmint (Mentha spicata) leaf, fresh
Handful lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) leaf, fresh
½ teaspoon lavender (Lavandula spp.) flower bud, optional
3 cups water
1 heaping tablespoon sweetener of choice (e.g., cane sugar, honey, maple syrup)

Directions

  • Place the lemon slices and herbs into a quart jar, add freshly boiled water, cover, and steep for 15 minutes.
  • When the water has cooled to room temperature, add the sweetener and stir well.
  • Strain out the herbs and reserve the liquid. Place it in the refrigerator until completely cool. 
  • Using a blender, crush ice and combine with the liquid, using a 2:1 ratio (3 cups of crushed ice to 1½ cups of liquid, for example). Blend well and enjoy! 

HYDRATING HERBAL ELECTROLYTE DRINKS

Hydrating herbal electrolyte drinks are a helpful and tasty addition to the summer drink lineup. Electrolytes are minerals like sodium, potassium, and magnesium that help your body stay hydrated, support muscle function, and maintain energy levels. In the summer heat, especially when sweating more than usual, replenishing electrolytes can help you stay balanced, refreshed, and ward off fatigue or dehydration. Drinks with a mix of natural sugars, salts, and fluids (like the two recipes below) are a simple, tasty way to support the body.

Hibiscus Citrus Electrolyte Drink

This vibrant red drink is bright, tart, and naturally refreshing—perfect for warm days when you need a little extra hydration. Hibiscus brings a tangy, fruity flavor and stunning color, while coconut water, citrus, and a pinch of sea salt offer gentle electrolyte support. Sip it chilled after time in the sun, a workout, or just because it tastes so good.

Ingredients

1 cup water
1 tablespoon hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa) calyx
1 cup coconut water
1 tablespoon raw honey (or maple syrup)
Juice of 1 orange (or lemon or lime)
⅛ teaspoon sea salt

Directions

  • Place hibiscus in a heatproof vessel. Boil water and pour over the herb. Cover and allow to steep for 10-15 minutes.
  • Once steeped, strain out the herb and let the infusion cool completely. 
  • Once cool, combine with the remaining ingredients until well blended. 
  • Store in the fridge for 1-2 days.
  • Serve chilled.

Cool and Calm Lemon Balm and Peppermint Electrolyte Drink 

Soothing and hydrating, the blend offers a mild citrusy-mint flavor and is crafted to help ease stress and support digestion.

Ingredients

1 cup water
½ tablespoon lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) leaf (or 1 tablespoon fresh)
½ tablespoon peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf (or 1 tablespoon fresh)
1 cup coconut water
1 tablespoon raw honey
Juice of 1 lemon or 1½ limes
⅛ teaspoon sea salt

Directions

  • Place lemon balm and peppermint in a heatproof vessel. Boil water and pour over the herbs. Cover and allow to steep for 10 minutes.
  • Once steeped, strain out the herbs and let the infusion cool completely. 
  • Once cool, combine with the remaining ingredients until well blended. 
  • Serve chilled.

This stunning botanical drink showcases the magic of butterfly pea flower (Clitoria ternatea), an herb known for its vibrant blue hue and color-changing properties. When exposed to acidic ingredients like lime juice, it transforms from deep blue to purple or pink, creating a beautiful visual experience.

Butterfly Pea Flower Botanical Drink with Botanical Lime Ice Cubes

To make this drink, butterfly pea flower syrup is prepared first, extracting the rich color and delicate floral notes of the flowers. This syrup is then combined with sparkling water, floral ice cubes, and fresh lime juice to create a refreshing and color-changing beverage perfect for summer gatherings, special occasions, or simply adding a touch of botanical beauty to your day.

Yield: One 8 fl oz glass

Ingredients

For Botanical Lime Ice Cubes

Lime juice, enough to fill a tray of ice cubes (pre-squeezed is easiest)
¼ cup lilac (Syringa vulgaris) flower (no stems), violet (Viola spp.) flower, or rose (Rosa spp.) petal

For Butterfly Pea Flower Syrup

½ cup water
½ cup cane sugar
3 tablespoons butterfly pea (Clitorea ternatea) flowers or 1 tablespoon butterfly pea flower powder

For Mint Sugar

3 tablespoons cane sugar or monkfruit granulated sweetener
3 tablespoons fresh mint (Mentha spp.) leaves

For Color-Changing Drink

¾ cup sparkling water
1 teaspoon butterfly pea flower syrup
2 botanical lime juice ice cubes
Additional lime juice to taste
1 teaspoon mint sugar for rim

Directions

First, make the Botanical Lime Ice Cubes

  • Place the flowers in an empty ice cube tray, and add the lime juice on top. 
  • Freeze completely and use 1-2 ice cubes per drink. 

Make the Butterfly Pea Flower Syrup

  • Warm the water over low heat and add the butterfly pea flowers. Bring to a near boil and turn down the heat, stirring in the sugar until dissolved. Remove the pan from the heat and cover with a lid.  
  • Steep for at least 20 minutes. 
  • Strain the mixture through a fine-mesh strainer, squeezing the butterfly pea flowers to get all the pigment out. Transfer syrup to a sanitized bottle and cap.
  • Label the bottle and use 1 teaspoon of this liquid to add flavor and color to ¾ cup of sparkling water. 

Make the Mint Sugar

  • Blend the sugar and fresh mint leaves in a coffee grinder or a spice grinder. Scoop the mixture out onto a small plate. 
  • After preparing the drink, leave any remaining mint sugar to dry completely, then scoop into a jar to save for another time. 

Prepare the Color-Changing Drink

  • Pour ½ teaspoon of the butterfly pea flower syrup onto a small plate, around the same circumference as your glass. 
  • Add the mint sugar to a separate small plate.
  • Roll the rim of the glass in the syrup, and then into the mint sugar, coating completely. 
  • Add the ice cubes, sparkling water, syrup, and lime juice to taste. Enjoy and watch as the color changes while the ice cubes melt.

Nourishing Oatstraw Infusion

Summer often brings a flurry of activity, heat, and long days that can wear on both the body and spirit. Oat (Avena sativa) offers calm in the chaos, offering mineral-rich nourishment and a grounded sense of ease.

Ingredients

1 cup oat (Avena sativa) straw
4 cups water

Directions

  • Place oatstraw in a heat-safe vessel.
  • Fill the container with boiling water, making sure the herb is fully covered.
  • Cover with a lid and steep at room temperature for 4-6 hours or overnight. 
  • Strain out the herbs. Preparing it in the evening and straining in the morning works well.
  • Enjoy over ice. Feel free to add a squeeze of lemon for a refreshing boost. 
  • Store infusion in the fridge and enjoy within 24 hours.

Summer Chill Lemonade

This refreshing lemonade blends the mineral-rich nourishment of oatstraw with the bright, uplifting notes of lemon balm. Lightly sweetened with raw honey and served over ice, it’s the perfect drink to enjoy on warm days when your body and mind need a little cooling support. 

Ingredients

2 tablespoons oat (Avena sativa) straw
2 tablespoons lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) leaf (or ½-¾ cup fresh)
2 cups boiling water
1 to 1½ cups raw local honey (adjust to taste)
2 cups fresh lemon juice (about 10–12 lemons)
Ice and cold filtered water to fill a 2-quart pitcher

Directions

  • In a heatproof jar or saucepan, combine oatstraw and lemon balm. Pour 2 cups of boiling water over the herbs.
  • Cover and let steep for 1-2 hours.
  • Strain the infusion into a large pitcher or quart jar.
  • Stir in the honey until fully dissolved.
  • Add lemon juice and stir.
  • Add ice and cold filtered water until you reach the 2 quart (8 cup) mark.
  • Stir well and serve over ice (botanical ice cubes would be lovely here!).
  • Garnish with fresh lemon balm, mint, or edible flowers for a finishing touch.

Lemon Balm and Mint Cool-Down Pops

These light and refreshing herbal ice pops are just the thing for a sweltering summer afternoon. Lemon balm brings a gentle citrusy calm, while mint adds a crisp, cooling lift. These are simple to make, naturally sweetened, and endlessly enjoyable! If you don’t have fresh lemon balm and mint, you can substitute 3 tablespoons dried of each. Safety note: If using honey, avoid serving to children under 1 year old. 

Ingredients

½ cup fresh lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) aerial parts
½ cup fresh mint (Mentha spp.) leaves 
4 cups water
1–2 tablespoon raw honey or maple syrup (to taste)
½ lime, juiced, optional
Lime, thinly sliced, optional
Ice pop molds

Directions

  • Place lemon balm and mint in a heatproof quart jar.
  • Pour boiling water over the herbs. Cover and steep for 15–20 minutes.
  • Strain the mixture through a fine-mesh strainer, reserving the liquid. Let the infusion cool to room temperature. Compost the herbs.
  • Stir in honey or maple syrup to taste. If using, add the lime juice and a few extra-thin lime slices.
  • Pour into popsicle molds and freeze for 4-6 hours or until solid.
  • Enjoy a calm, cooling treat that tastes like sunshine in a pop!

Blueberry Basil Frozen Yogurt with Blueberry Compote Swirl

A creamy, cooling treat with the sweet tang of blueberries and a hint of fresh basil—perfect for a summer afternoon pick-me-up. For a subtle floral twist, try using lavender in place of the basil, or combine the two for a uniquely refreshing flavor. Safety note: If using honey, avoid serving to children under 1 year old. 

Ingredients

For blueberry compote

½ cup fresh or frozen blueberries
1 tablespoon honey or maple syrup
Splash of lemon juice, optional
Pinch of sea salt, optional

For frozen yogurt base
1 cup plain yogurt (dairy or dairy-free)
1 pound frozen blueberries
2 tablespoon honey or maple syrup (or to taste)
1-3 teaspoon fresh basil leaves, lightly chopped

Directions

Make the compote (can be made ahead)

  • In a small saucepan, combine the ½ cup blueberries and 1 tablespoon sweetener.
  • Cook over medium heat until the berries burst and the mixture thickens slightly, 5-7 minutes.
  • Optional: add a squeeze of lemon juice or a pinch of salt to brighten up the flavor.
  • Let cool completely.

Make the frozen yogurt

  • Add the yogurt, frozen blueberries, basil, and sweetener to a high-speed blender or food processor.
  • Pulse until ingredients begin to combine. Scrape down the sides as needed, and use a tamper if your blender has one to push the blueberries toward the blades and help incorporate everything evenly. Continue pulsing until smooth and creamy.
  • For soft-serve frozen yogurt, gently spoon the compote into the frozen yogurt and use a knife or spatula to swirl it through before serving.
  • For a firmer treat, transfer the frozen yogurt to a container, spoon in dollops of the compote, swirl with a butter knife, and freeze for 1–2 hours until scoopable.

Creamy Lemon Balm Peach Mango Nice Cream

This refreshing, no-churn frozen treat is bursting with summer flavor. Sweet mango, juicy peach, and soothing lemon balm blend into a smooth, creamy dessert with no added sugar or dairy—just pure, plant-based goodness.

Ingredients

3 frozen bananas
1 cup frozen mango
1 cup frozen peaches
¾ cup fresh lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) leaves
½ cup coconut milk
Sprigs of lemon balm (for garnish, optional)
Fresh berries (optional, for garnish)

Directions

  • Place all ingredients in a food processor or high-speed blender.
  • Blend until smooth and creamy, scraping down the sides as needed. Add a splash more coconut milk if the mixture is too thick.
  • Serve immediately or transfer to a freezer-safe container and freeze 1-2 hours for a firmer scoop.
  • Garnish with a lemon balm sprig or fresh berries, if desired.

Strawberry Mint Sorbet

This refreshing sorbet blends ripe strawberries with a hint of mint for a cool, lightly sweetened treat. It’s delightful as an afternoon or evening pick-me-up and makes a beautiful finish to a summer meal. Safety note: Due to honey, avoid serving to children under 1 year old. 

Ingredients

3 cups strawberries, chopped (the riper the berry, the sweeter the sorbet)
¼ cup honey
1 teaspoon mint (Mentha spp.) leaf (or 2 teaspoons fresh)

Directions

  • Place strawberries, honey, and mint into a blender and blend until smooth on a high setting. It may be necessary to start the blender on a low speed and work up to a higher speed in order to get the mixture to liquefy properly without adding water to the mixture, so use your best judgment. Use a tamper if your blender has one to push the mixture toward the blades and help incorporate everything evenly.
  • Once the fruit, honey, and mint have reached a smooth purée, transfer into a small container and refrigerate overnight. This allows the flavors time to marry and ensures that the purée is chilled before churning.
  • Transfer the chilled purée into your ice cream mixer and, following the instructions for your ice cream maker, start the churning process. Once the contents have reached a soft serve consistency, you can either serve or put the soft sorbet in a freezer-safe container overnight for a firmer scoop. 

Botanical Ice Cubes

Botanical ice cubes are a beautiful and refreshing way to infuse drinks with subtle flavors and visual appeal. Made with edible flowers, herbs, and even fruit, these cubes elevate water, tea, cocktails, and mocktails.

Ingredients

Filtered water
Fresh edible flowers, like rose (Rosa spp.), lavender (Lavandula spp. ), violet (Viola spp.), pansy (Viola x wittrockiana), nasturtium (Tropaeolum majus), chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla), or calendula (Calendula officinalis)
Fresh herbs, such as mint (Mentha spp.), basil (Ocimum basilicum), rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus), or lemon balm (Melissa officinalis)
Ice cube tray 

Directions

  • Gently rinse flowers and herbs, removing any tough stems.
  • Pour filtered water into the ice cube tray. Fill about halfway.
  • Place flowers or herbs in each cube, ensuring they lay as flat as possible.
  • Freeze for about 1 hour until the water is semi-solid. This helps keep the botanicals from floating to the top.
  • Fill the rest of each cube with water and freeze completely (about 4-6 hours).
  • Once frozen, pop out the cubes and store them in an airtight container in the freezer until needed.

Notes

  • Add thin slices of lemon, lime, or orange for a bright citrus burst.
  • Freeze small berries like raspberries or blueberries with herbs for a pretty and flavorful addition to drinks.
  • Herbal infusions also make wonderful ice cubes, adding a flavorful twist to your favorite drinks. Simply brew a strong herbal infusion, let it cool, and freeze it in ice cube trays. These herbal ice cubes bring a burst of flavor to sparkling water, iced tea, lemonade, or cocktails, and they blend beautifully into smoothies for extra herbal benefits. Try hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa), ice cubes for a tangy splash or chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) cubes for a calming, floral touch, lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) for an uplifting, citrusy zing, or mint (Mentha spp.) for delightful refreshment.

Botanical Salads + Dressings

In this section, we’ll explore the vibrant, flavorful world of plant-filled salads and herbal dressings—perfect for making the most of the summer garden. With fresh greens, aromatic herbs, and edible flowers at your fingertips, even a simple salad can become a colorful, nourishing feast. Here, you’ll find ideas and recipes that highlight the beauty and versatility of herbs and blooms in your kitchen. From petal-strewn garden salads to zesty citrus dressings and herb-infused condiments, these creations bring seasonal plants to the table in fresh, creative ways.

Edible Flowers

Edible flowers are abundant throughout the summer months, offering a delightful way to connect with the plants and infuse meals with seasonal charm. These delicate petals can be peppery, citrusy, floral, or slightly sweet, making them a playful and beautiful ingredient in both savory and sweet dishes. Whether layered into a salad, blended into a dressing, or scattered on top as a final flourish, edible flowers bring a fresh, seasonal twist to your summer table.

Below is a list of just a few of the most common edible flowers you can safely use in salads, dressings, butters, and more—each offering its own unique flavor, texture, and visual appeal. We’ve noted the part of the flower typically used; of course you could place whole flowers for display, but smaller parts of flowers may be best for each bite!

  • Bee balm (Monarda fistulosa, M. didyma) petal
  • Blue cornflower (Centaurea cyanus) petal
  • California poppy (Escholschzia californica) petal
  • Calendula (Calendula officinalis) petal
  • Chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) flower
  • Chive (Allium schoenoprasum) floret
  • Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale) ray floret
  • Hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa) calyx
  • Lavender (Lavandula spp.) flower or flower bud
  • Nasturtium (Tropaeolum majus) flower
  • Pansy (Viola × wittrockiana) flower
  • Red clover (Trifolium pratense) floret
  • Rose (Rosa spp.) petal
  • Rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) flower
  • Sourgrass (Oxalis stricta) flower
  • Violet (Viola spp.) flower

Summer Garden Salad with Edible Flowers and Fresh Herbs

This vibrant summer salad is bursting with color, flavor, and freshness. Featuring crisp greens, fragrant herbs, and delicate edible flowers, it’s a light and refreshing dish perfect for warm-weather meals. Customize this salad to suit your taste preferences and incorporate the fresh edible flowers and herbs around you. 

Ingredients

Salad Base

4 cups mixed greens such as butter lettuce, arugula, baby spinach, mesclun mix, or wild edible greens like chickweed (Stellaria media) or miner’s lettuce (Claytonia perfoliata)
½ cup cherry tomatoes, halved
½ cup cucumber, thinly sliced
¼ cup radishes, thinly sliced
¼ cup fresh berries (strawberries, blueberries, or raspberries)

Fresh Herbs and Edible Flowers
¼ cup fresh basil (Ocimum basilicum), torn
2 tablespoons fresh mint (Mentha spp.), chopped
¼ cup edible flowers (see list above)

Optional Add-ins
¼ cup crumbled goat cheese or feta
¼ cup toasted nuts or seeds (almonds, sunflower seeds, or pistachios)
Avocado slices
Dressing of choice (see options in recipes further below)

Directions

  • In a large bowl, combine mixed greens, cherry tomatoes, cucumber, radishes, and berries.
  • Sprinkle fresh basil, mint, chives, and edible flowers over the top.
  • Drizzle with dressing of choice and gently toss to coat.
  • Serve immediately as a refreshing summer dish on its own or alongside grilled fish, chicken, or a light pasta.

Citrus and Nasturtium Summer Salad 

This unique summer salad combines the bold, peppery flavor of nasturtium with bright citrus for a light and refreshing dish. The vibrant blooms add both beauty and a subtle spice, making it a perfect addition to warm-weather meals.

Ingredients

For salad base
Mixed greens such as arugula, butter lettuce, baby spinach, mesclun, chickweed, and/or miner’s lettuce)]
Orange and grapefruit segments
Thinly sliced fennel or cucumber
Toasted almonds or pecans
Crumbled goat cheese or feta (optional)
Nasturtium flowers and leaves

For dressing
3 tablespoons olive oil
2 tablespoons fresh orange juice
1 teaspoon honey (or maple syrup)
1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
Salt and pepper, to taste

Directions

  • Wash and dry mixed greens and place in a large bowl.
  • Peel and segment oranges and grapefruit, removing any seeds and membranes.
  • Thinly slice fennel or cucumber and scatter over the greens.
  • Sprinkle toasted almonds or pecans over the salad for a nutty crunch.
  • Crumble goat cheese or feta over the salad, if using. 
  • Tear nasturtium leaves into bite-sized pieces and sprinkle both the leaves and flowers over the salad.
  • To prepare the dressing, whisk together olive oil, fresh orange juice, honey, Dijon mustard, salt, and pepper to taste.
  • Drizzle the dressing over the salad and gently toss to coat.
  • Serve immediately and enjoy the refreshing balance of sweet citrus, spicy nasturtiums, and crisp greens.

Herbal Summer Fruit Salad

This refreshing fruit salad is a celebration of summer’s sweetness, made even more vibrant with fresh herbs and edible flowers. This dish is as beautiful as it is delicious—perfect for picnics, brunches, or a simple afternoon treat.

Ingredients

3 cups mixed fresh fruit, such as:
Strawberries, sliced
Blueberries
Peaches or nectarines, sliced
Watermelon or cantaloupe, cubed
Grapes, halved
¼ cup fresh mint (Mentha spp.) leaf, chopped
2 tablespoons fresh basil (Ocimum basilicum) or lemon balm (Melissa officinalis), finely sliced
1 lime or ½ lemon, juiced
1 tablespoon raw honey or maple syrup (optional, for added sweetness)
¼ teaspoon orange zest
Bee balm (Monarda fistulosa, M. didyma) petal, calendula (Calendula officinalis) petal, chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) flower, or violet (Viola spp.) flower for garnish

Directions

  • Wash, slice, and combine all fruit in a large mixing bowl.
  • Sprinkle chopped mint and basil over the fruit.
  • In a small bowl, whisk together lime or lemon juice, honey (if using), and orange zest.
  • Pour the dressing over the fruit, gently toss to combine, and let the salad sit for 10-15 minutes in the fridge to allow the flavors to blend.
  • Serve and enjoy! Garnish with additional herbs and edible flowers like violet, bee balm, or chamomile for a beautiful finishing touch.

Citrus Herb Dressing

Bright, fresh, and full of flavor, this simple citrus herb dressing is a versatile staple for summer meals. The combination of lemon, olive oil, and fresh herbs brings a zesty, uplifting note to salads, grain bowls, grilled veggies, and more.

Ingredients

3 tablespoons extra virgin olive oil
1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
1 teaspoon honey or maple syrup
1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
1 tablespoon fresh herbs, such as thyme (Thymus vulgaris), parsley (Petroselinum crispum), or basil (Ocimum basilicum), finely chopped
Salt and pepper, to taste

Directions

  • Add olive oil, lemon juice, honey, and Dijon mustard to a small jar or bowl and whisk to combine. 
  • Add fresh herbs and stir to combine. 
  • Season with salt and pepper to taste.

Herbie Yogurt Dressing

This cooling, creamy dressing blends full-fat yogurt with garlic, lemon, and fresh herbs for a delightful, herbaceous flavor. Perfect for cabbage salads, grain bowls, or as a dip for veggies.

Ingredients

½ cup full-fat, plain yogurt
⅛ cup chopped fresh curly parsley (Petroselinum crispum) leaves, mint (Mentha x piperita) leaves, dill (Anethum graveolens) or cilantro (Coriandrum sativum) leaves
1 clove chopped and peeled garlic
1-2 teaspoons lemon juice
⅛ teaspoon ground black pepper (Piper nigrum) fruit
Salt to taste

Directions

  • Add all ingredients to a blender or food processor and blend until smooth and evenly combined.
  • If you don’t have a blender, simply finely chop the herbs and garlic, then add all ingredients to a tightly sealed glass jar and shake well to combine.

Vegan Green Goddess Dressing

A garden-fresh and versatile dressing that’s completely dairy-free. This version blends parsley, basil, dill, and tarragon with creamy tahini and lemon juice. It’s perfect for drizzling over greens, grain bowls, or roasted veggies—and it also makes a flavorful dip!

Ingredients

¾ cup packed fresh parsley (Petroselinum crispum) leaves
¾ cup packed fresh basil (Ocimum basilicum) leaves
¼ cup packed fresh dill (Anethum graveolens) leaves, or 1-2 teaspoons dried leaf
1 tablespoons chopped fresh tarragon (Artemisia dracunculus) leaves
½ green onion, trimmed and chopped
1 garlic clove, peeled
½ teaspoon salt
¼ cup tahini
2 tablespoons lemon juice
⅛-½ cup cold water (to reach desired consistency)

Directions

  • In a food processor, combine parsley, basil, dill, tarragon, green onion, garlic, and salt.
  • Pulse until everything is finely chopped.
  • Scrape down the sides and add the tahini and lemon juice. Blend again until it forms a thick, creamy paste.
  • With the processor running, slowly drizzle in cold water through the top. Start with ¼ cup and add more, a little at a time, until the dressing reaches your desired consistency (use less water for a dip-like texture, and more for a pourable dressing).
  • Taste and adjust, adding more lemon, salt, or herbs if needed. If the flavor of tarragon is too subtle, you can stir in an extra teaspoon, finely chopped, at the end.
  • Transfer to a jar with a tight-fitting lid. Store in the fridge for up to 3 days. Shake or stir before each use.

Herbal Condiments

Herbal condiments are a fun and flavorful way to bring herbs into everyday meals. Whether you’re blending up a nutrient-packed pesto, drizzling chimichurri over grilled veggies, or mixing blossoms into butter or salt, these simple recipes highlight the fresh, seasonal abundance of the plant world. They’re quick to make, easy to adapt, and perfect for adding an herbal boost to summer dishes.

Any Greens Pesto 

Pesto isn’t just for basil and pine nuts anymore. It’s a method—a versatile blend of greens, herbs, nuts or seeds, garlic, oil, and acid—that can be adapted to whatever’s growing in your garden, showing up at the farmer’s market, or popping up wild in your backyard. This is where weedy, nutrient-dense plants like nettle, chickweed, lambsquarter, and purslane really shine. When paired with familiar garden greens like spinach or arugula, and rounded out with flavorful herbs, you get a nourishing, vibrant pesto that’s as good for your body as it is for your taste buds.

Not only is this a great way to reduce food waste and eat seasonally, it’s also budget-friendly—skip the expensive pine nuts and use sunflower seeds, pumpkin seeds, or walnuts instead. This formula invites you to mix, match, and make it your own.

Ingredients

1 cup “weedy” edible greens such as nettle, chickweed, lambsquarter, purslane, dandelion greens, violet leaf, etc.
1 cup garden greens like spinach, arugula, kale, beet greens, chard, or even carrot tops
½-1 cup fresh herbs such as basil, parsley, cilantro, dill, sage, tulsi, chives
½ cup nuts or seeds, such as sunflower seeds, pumpkin seeds, hemp seeds, walnuts, almonds, pistachios, cashews, or pine nuts
2-3 cloves garlic (or ¼-½ cup fresh garlic scapes)
2-4 tablespoons lemon juice
½ cup olive oil (more to thin, if needed)
Salt to taste
2-4 tablespoons Parmesan, Asiago, or Romano cheese or nutritional yeast (for dairy-free), optional

Directions

  • Blanch or steam tougher greens or herbs that require special preparation (such as stinging nettle to remove the sting—blending thoroughly also removes the sting, but can also blanch to ensure a smooth taste!)
  • Lightly toast nuts in a dry pan until fragrant. Remove from heat and set aside to cool.
  • Add greens, herbs, nuts/seeds, garlic, lemon juice, and salt to a food processor or blender. Pulse until roughly combined. 
  • Drizzle in olive oil while blending and blend until you reach desired consistency.
  • Add cheese or nutritional yeast, if using. Pulse until cheese or nutritional yeast is incorporated. 
  • Taste and add extra salt or cheese if desired. If pesto is too thick, add more oil, if too thin, add more garden greens or herbs and blend until desired consistency is reached. You want bright, bold flavor and a texture that spreads or stirs easily.
  • Serve over pasta, meats, grain bowls, roasted veggies, sandwiches, eggs, pizza, or stir into soup!

Originally from Argentina, chimichurri is a vibrant and savory Latin American sauce traditionally served with grilled meats. A staple of the asado—Argentina’s iconic barbecue—it’s drizzled over grilled meat seasoned simply with salt and pepper. With the brightness of fresh herbs and the bold, pungent bite of garlic and onion, it has warming, carminative qualities that not only elevate your meal but also support healthy digestion.

Bright, bold, and packed with flavor, chimichurri is a summertime staple! Join Herbal Academy team member Cristina Asensio Foley as she shares her family’s take on this classic Argentinian sauce and walks you through how to make your own.

Chimichurri

This chimichurri recipe is Cristina’s family favorite. Feel free to adapt by swapping cilantro for oregano or extra parsley, or tryother fresh herbs, like basil or spearmint.  For a spicy twist, added chilies or jalapeños. Chimichurri is most famously paired with meat, but is also delicious on grilled vegetables, seafood, rice, empanadas, and more.

Ingredients

1 cup fresh Italian parsley (Petroselinum crispum var. neapolitanum), chopped 
1 cup fresh green onion, chopped
¼ cup fresh cilantro, chopped
4 garlic cloves
¼  onion
2 cups olive oil
½ a lime, juiced
1 teaspoon of white vinegar
1-2 teaspoon salt, adjust to taste

Directions

  • Place all ingredients into a blender and blend until desired consistency is achieved. 
  • Alternatively, finely dice onion and garlic, then add all ingredients to a glass jar and mix.
  • Store in the fridge for up to 1 week.
  • Serve with any dish and enjoy!

Blossom Butter

Blossom butter is a delightful showstopper! It’ll be our little secret that it’s actually made very easily and quickly—just mix a handful of edible flowers with a piece of room-temperature butter, some grated lemon zest, and salt.

Ingredients

1 cup unsalted organic grass-fed butter (at room temperature)
¼ cup fresh herbs and flowers of choice (see edible flower list above for options)
1 teaspoon salt of choice
zest of ½ lemon
1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice 

Directions

  • Rinse and dry your chosen herbs and flowers, then finely cut or pluck them into smaller pieces.
  • In a large bowl, combine the herbs and blossoms with the soft butter, salt, lemon zest, and juice, using a spatula or spoon.
  • Transfer the mixed butter into a butter dish or onto a sheet of parchment paper. If choosing the parchment paper, roll up to form a cylinder. Twist both ends tight and refrigerate for at least one hour. The butter is ready to serve when cold and solid.
  • Butter will last in the refrigerator for about a week.

Herbal Finishing Salts

Many recipes suggest simply adding salt and pepper to taste or finishing with a sprinkle of sea salt. But what if we took it a step further and incorporated herbs with every final touch? Not only would this enhance the flavor of our dishes, but it would also add depth and complexity. Culinary herbal finishing salts are incredibly simple to make and surprisingly underutilized!

Fresh Flower Finishing Salt

A delicate, fragrant flower-infused salt adds beauty and flavor to any dish. Perfect for sprinkling over salads, roasted vegetables, grilled fish, or even desserts!

Ingredients

½ cup sea salt
¼ cup fresh edible flower petals such as butterfly pea (Clitoria ternatea), rose (Rosa spp.), calendula (Calendula officinalis), lavender (Lavandula spp.), nasturtium (Tropaeolum majus), or violet (Viola spp.)
½ teaspoon herbs, such as such as thyme (Thymus vulgaris), rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus), or lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) for extra flavor (optional)

Directions

  • Gently rinse and pat dry the flower petals. Remove any tough stems or bitter parts.
  • In a food processor or mortar and pestle, pulse the salt and flower petals together until combined and slightly colored by the flowers.
  • Spread the mixture evenly on a baking sheet lined with parchment paper. Bake at 200°F (93°C) for 10-15 minutes, or until the petals are fully dried. Stir halfway through to ensure even drying.
  • Once dry, remove from the oven and add dried herbs if using. Stir to combine. 
  • Let the salt cool completely before transferring it to an airtight jar. 
  • Store in a cool, dry place.
  • Sprinkle over your favorite dishes for a touch of floral elegance!

Variations and Uses

  • Rose (Rosa spp.) and lavender (Lavandula spp.)–perfect for sprinkling over dark chocolate or shortbread.
  • Calendula (Calendula officinalis) and thyme (Thymus vulgaris)–a great addition to roasted vegetables or grilled chicken. 
  • Lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) and violet (Viola spp.)–bright and citrusy, ideal for seafood and salads.
  • Nasturtium (Tropaeolum majus) and red chili pepper (Capsicum annuum) flakes–a spicy floral salt for adding heat to dishes.

Rose and Chamomile Floral Finishing Salt

Sprinkle over desserts, chocolate, or even a floral-infused cocktail rim.

Ingredients
⅛ cup flaky sea salt
1 tablespoon rose (Rosa spp.) petal
1 teaspoon chamomile (Matricaria chamomilla) flower

Directions

  • Powder rose petals and chamomile in a spice grinder.
  • Combine powdered rose and chamomile with flaky salt in a spice grinder, clean coffee grinder, small food processor, or mortar and pestle. 
  • Pulse or grind briefly to break down the salt slightly and combine with the herbs, but keep some texture.
  • Transfer to a jar with a tight-fitting lid. Store in a cool, dry place for up to 6 months.

The following finishing salts use dried herbs, are quick to prepare, and add a flavorful punch to a variety of meals. These salts are incredibly adaptable and can be used to elevate everything from grilled veggies to fruit salads and even desserts.

Lemon Balm and Calendula Citrus Salt

Sprinkle over grilled fish, roasted veggies, or summer salads for a bright, citrusy kick.

Ingredients

⅛ cup coarse sea salt
1 tablespoon lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) aerial parts
1 tablespoon calendula (Calendula officinalis) petal
Zest of 1 lemon

Directions

  • Combine ingredients in a spice grinder, coffee grinder, or small food processor.
  • Pulse or grind briefly to break down herbs and incorporate flavors, but keep some texture.
  • Spread the mixture on a parchment-lined tray and let it air-dry for several hours until it feels completely dry.
  • Transfer to a jar with a tight-fitting lid. Store in a cool, dry place for up to 6 months.

Hibiscus-Mint Salt

Enhances fresh watermelon, cucumber slices, or fruit salads with a tangy, cooling effect.

Ingredients
⅛ cup coarse sea salt
1½ tablespoons hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa) calyx
1½ tablespoons spearmint (Mentha spicata) or peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf

Directions

  • Powder hibiscus and mint in a spice grinder.
  • Combine powdered herbs and salt in a spice grinder, clean coffee grinder, or small food processor.
  • Pulse or grind briefly to break down the salt and combine with the herbs, but keep some texture.
  • Transfer to a jar with a tight-fitting lid. Store in a cool, dry place for up to 6 months.

Basil and Garlic Summer Herb Salt

Perfect for seasoning caprese salads, pasta dishes, and grilled corn.

Ingredients
⅛ cup coarse salt
1 tablespoon basil (Ocimum basilicum) leaf
½ teaspoon garlic (Allium sativum) powder
Zest of 1 lime

Directions

  • Combine ingredients in a spice grinder, coffee grinder, or small food processor.
  • Pulse briefly to break down herbs and incorporate flavors, but keep some texture.
  • Spread the mixture on a parchment-lined tray and let it air-dry for several hours until it feels completely dry.
  • Transfer to a jar with a tight-fitting lid. Store in a cool, dry place for up to 6 months.

Conclusion

As we wrap up this flavorful exploration of summer herbs, remember that cooking with herbs is about more than just taste—it’s about creativity, seasonal connection, and simple joy. Each time you snip fresh basil, muddle mint, or drizzle an herbal-infused oil, you’re engaging in a sensory experience that brings nourishment and beauty to the everyday.

Let these recipes and ideas be a jumping-off point. Keep experimenting with new flavor combinations, trying different preparations, and sharing your creations with others. With a basket of fresh herbs and a bit of inspiration, your summer kitchen can become a space of discovery, delight, and connection with the vibrant world around you!
