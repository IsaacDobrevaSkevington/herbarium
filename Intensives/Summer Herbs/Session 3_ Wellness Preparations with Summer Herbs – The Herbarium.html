Session 3: Wellness Preparations with Summer Herbs

60 minutes

Introduction

Summer brings a unique set of challenges and opportunities for the body. The warmth and sunny nature of the season can be invigorating, encouraging us to spend more time outdoors and embrace a more active lifestyle. Yet, this increased exposure to heat, sun, and insects—as well as a tendency toward dehydration and occasional skin irritation—means our bodies may need a bit of extra support to stay comfortable and balanced.

Many of the plants that flourish during this time of year offer cooling, soothing, and skin-supportive properties that feel especially welcome in the heat. These herbs can be worked into everyday routines, whether through sprays that refresh hot skin, teas that cool from within, or preparations that ease common summer discomforts like sun exposure or bug bites.

In this lesson, we’ll focus on practical, hands-on ways to work with summer herbs so you feel your best. You’ll find ideas for crafting facial mists, tea blends, topical sprays, and more—each designed to help you stay cool, comfortable, and connected to the natural rhythms of the season.

Cool and Refresh

When summer heat sets in, our bodies instinctively seek ways to cool down—whether it’s reaching for a cold drink, seeking shade, or jumping into a lake. Herbs can also play a beautiful role in offering that sense of refreshment. As we discussed in Session 1, many summer plants have naturally cooling, calming qualities that can support the body’s efforts to stay balanced in hot, active weather. This section offers simple ways to bring those benefits into your daily rhythm.

Calendula Facial Mist

The bursting joy of the vibrant calendula flower can be bottled up and enjoyed as a botanical hydrosol in just a few easy steps. Hydrosols are distilled to extract the aromatic molecules and volatile oils from flowers or herbs. This process creates a fragrant, lovely floral water, perfect for natural skincare or aromatherapy, and can be used in various skin and hair care products. This botanical hydrosol is perfect for a facial toner, body mist, skin soother, and more. 

Ingredients

Large handful of fresh calendula (Calendula officinalis) flowers
2 cups distilled water
Ice 
Sandwich bag

Directions

  • Place a ramekin, small bowl, or small plate upside down inside a large pot.
  • Place small bowl on top of the ramekin/plate.
  • Sprinkle fresh calendula in the pot and around the bowl (do not cover the bowl).
  • Pour about 2 cups of water into the pot, but do not submerge the bowl.
  • Place the pot lid upside down on the pot.
  • Place a freezer bag filled with ice on the lid.
  • Bring water to a simmer for about 20-30 minutes.
  • Change out sandwich bag of ice as it melts.
  • Turn off heat and carefully remove the lid.
  • Use clean oven mitts or silicone grip to remove the bowl of hydrosol liquid.
  • Bottle hydrosol and store in the refrigerator for up to 6 months.

Cooling Body Spray with Herb-Infused Witch Hazel Hydrosol

This refreshing herbal body spray is perfect for hot summer days, post-sun care, or anytime your skin needs a cooling boost. Featuring a soothing infusion of calendula and rose in witch hazel, paired with mint hydrosol, it tones, calms, and revitalizes the skin with every spritz. Keep it in the fridge for an extra burst of chill!

Ingredients

1 tablespoon calendula (Calendula officinalis) petals
1 tablespoon rose (Rosa spp.) petals
2 oz witch hazel hydrosol
3 oz peppermint (Mentha x piperita) or spearmint (Mentha spicata) hydrosol

Directions

  • Combine calendula and rose petals in a clean 4 oz glass jar.
  • Fill the jar to the top with witch hazel extract, making sure herbs are completely covered.
  • Seal with a lid, shake gently, and store in a cool, dark place to infuse for 2-4 weeks.
  • Strain the herbs using a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth, pressing to extract all liquid.
  • Label and store the infused witch hazel in a clean glass jar.
  • To make the body spray, add 1 oz of infused witch hazel to a 4 oz spray bottle.
  • Add 3 oz of mint hydrosol.yar
  • If desired, add aloe vera gel for added cooling and skin support.
  • Shake well before each use.
  • Store in the refrigerator for extra refreshment and use within 6 months.
  • Spritz onto arms, neck, chest, or legs whenever you need to cool down and refresh.

Cool-Down Tea for Hot Summer Days

This tea blend features yarrow to support the body’s natural ability to regulate heat, lemon balm and peppermint for their cooling and refreshing qualities, and hibiscus for a tart, mineral-rich boost.

Ingredients

2 teaspoons lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) leaf
2 teaspoons peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf
1 teaspoon yarrow (Achillea millefolium) aerial parts
1 teaspoon hibiscus (Hibiscus sabdariffa) calyx
2 cups water

Directions

  • Combine herbs in a glass quart jar (4 cup capacity).
  • Pour 2 cups of boiling water over the mixture, cover, and steep for 1 hour.
  • Strain herbs by pouring the mixture through a tea strainer. Compost the herbs.
  • Place the infusion in a labeled wide-mouth glass jar and store in the refrigerator for up to 24 hours.
  • Add honey to sweeten, if desired, and a squeeze of lemon or lime juice for a refreshing twist.
  • Enjoy over ice.

Cool Down Calendula Mint Ice Cubes for Topical Application

Cool off fast with these refreshing herbal cubes, perfect for sun-soaked skin, hot flashes, or just a sweltering summer day. Packed with soothing, skin-loving herbs, this simple recipe uses natural ingredients to beat the heat with a burst of botanical refreshment whenever your skin needs it. 

Ingredients

2 tablespoon calendula (Calendula officinalis) flower
1 tablespoon peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf
1 cup distilled water 

Optional add-ins after steeping
¼ cup cucumber juice or hydrosol, if desired
¼ cup aloe vera juice (not gel), if desired

Directions

  • Add all dried herbs to a heat-safe vessel and pour boiling water over the herbs. Cover and let steep for 30-60 minutes for a strong, concentrated infusion.
  • Once the steeping is complete, strain the infusion through a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth. Press gently to extract as much liquid as possible. Let the infusion cool to room temperature, or place it in the fridge (covered) to speed up the process.
  • Add any desired optional ingredients, if using. Stir until well combined. 
  • Pour the cooled herbal liquid into an ice cube tray. Place in the freezer until solid (several hours or overnight). Once frozen, transfer cubes to a labeled freezer-safe container or Ziploc bag and store in freezer. Use within 3 months for best results.

To Use

Remove one herbal cube from the tray and gently glide the cube over sunburned, flushed, or irritated areas—especially the back of the neck, shoulders, and chest. Use after a day in the sun, during a hot flash, or whenever your skin needs a quick cool-down.

Itch Ease Ice Cubes for Topical Application

If you’ve ever had a run-in with poison ivy, you know how stubborn the itch can be. Thankfully, nature offers gentle yet effective support through herbs like jewelweed and plantain—both known for their soothing, skin-calming properties. This easy recipe turns fresh herbs into cooling ice cubes you can keep on hand for instant respite. Rub a frozen cube directly on itchy or irritated skin to help ease discomfort and support skin health. While jewelweed and plantain work beautifully together, feel free to use one or the other if that’s what you have on hand.

Ingredients

Fresh jewelweed (Impatiens capensis) aerial parts
Fresh plantain (Plantango spp.) leaf
Distilled water

Directions

  • Gather fresh jewelweed aerial parts and plantain leaves. Rinse to remove dirt or bugs.
  • Chop the jewelweed and plantain into smaller pieces and place in a blender. Add just enough water to get everything moving—start with ¼ to ½ cup and increase as needed.
  • Once blended to a juicy pulp, strain through a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth, collecting the liquid in a clean bowl.
  • Pour the jewelweed-plantain juice into an ice cube tray and freeze until solid. Transfer frozen cubes to an airtight container or freezer bag and store in freezer. Use within 3 months for best results..

To Use

  • Rub a frozen cube directly over itchy, irritated skin.
  • Or, thaw a cube in the fridge and apply the liquid with a cotton pad or cloth.

Cooling Yarrow Compress for Overheating

Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) is a powerful herbal ally known for its ability to regulate blood flow and help the body release excess heat. This makes it an excellent choice for those hot summer days when you’re feeling overheated or flushed or have spent time in the sun.

Ingredients

3-4 tablespoons yarrow (Achillea millefolium) aerial parts
1 cup water
Clean cloth (cotton or linen works best)
Bowl for straining

Directions

  • Add yarrow to a heat-safe vessel and pour boiling water over the herb. Cover and let steep for 30-–60 minutes for a strong, concentrated infusion.
  • Once the steeping is complete, strain the infusion through a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth. Press gently to extract as much liquid as possible. Let the infusion cool to room temperature, or place it in the fridge (covered) to speed up the process.

To Use

Drench a clean cloth in the cooled liquid, then wring out the excess. Place the compress on areas where you feel overheated—forehead, back of the neck, chest, or ankles. Re-soak the cloth as needed when it warms up. Apply the compress for 10-15 minutes or as needed for cooling and calming. 

Tip

For sunburns, peppermint is another excellent herb to use in a compress. Its cooling menthol content can ease hot, inflamed skin. Just be sure to test for sensitivity, as peppermint on the skin can cause tingling.

Summer Skin Support

While summer invites us to be more active and connected with nature, it can also bring increased exposure to heat, sun, wind, and insects. Fortunately, many summer herbs offer gentle support for skin that’s feeling dry, flushed, or irritated.

In this section, you’ll find a collection of soothing herbal preparations designed to comfort and care for summer skin. From vinegar-infused baths and after-sun sprays to salves, succus cubes, and herbal soaks, these recipes can help you wind down, nourish your skin, and restore a sense of ease after time outdoors.

AFTER SUN CARE

Cooling Rose Mint Vinegar Bath 

This cooling botanical bath is a simple way to soothe sun-exposed skin using the refreshing properties of mint and the gentle touch of rose. Apple cider vinegar helps balance the skin’s pH, while the herbs lend their cooling, comforting magic. Add this infusion to your bath for a naturally nourishing soak that leaves you feeling refreshed from head to toe.

Ingredients

4 cups apple cider vinegar
½ cup rose (Rosa spp.) petal (or 1 cup fresh rose petal)
½ cup peppermint (Mentha x piperita) or spearmint (Mentha spicata) leaf (or 1 cup fresh mint leaf)

Directions

  • Place herbs in a sanitized quart jar.
  • Pour apple cider vinegar over the herbs until they are well covered and vinegar is at least an inch above the top of the herbs. Stir to combine.
  • Place a square piece of parchment paper on top of the jar, then seal the jar with a lid (this protects the extract from any chemical coating that may be on the lid, and protects the lid from rusting when using vinegar).
  • Set jar in a cool, dark place and let it sit for at least a week (2-4 weeks is optimal). Gently rotate jar every few days to mix herbs and vinegar.
  • Strain herbs from vinegar using a fine mesh strainer lined with cheesecloth. Gather ends of cheesecloth and squeeze herbs with your hands to release every last drop of vinegar. 
  • Bottle the vinegar and don’t forget to add a label.
  • Store in a cool, dark place for up to 6 months if using dried herbs (if using fresh herbs, shelf life will be shorter). 
  • Add 1-2 cups of infused vinegar to bath water. Soak and enjoy!

Sol Soother (After Sun Spray) by Felicia Cocotzin Ruiz

This soothing after-sun spray recipe comes from Felicia Cocotzin Ruiz’s book, Earth Medicines: Ancestral Wisdom, Healing Recipes, and Wellness Rituals from a Curandera. Made with fresh aloe and calming botanicals, it helps cool and nourish sun-kissed skin.

Yield: Makes about 1 cup

Ingredients

1 cup water
¼ cup lavender (Lavandula angustifolia) flower bud
¼ cup thick aloe vera (Aloe vera) gel 
10 drops geranium (Pelargonium graveolens) essential oil
¼ teaspoon vitamin E oil (optional)
8 fl oz dark glass bottle with spray top

Directions

  • Bring the water to boil in a small saucepan. Turn off the heat and add the lavender flowers. 
  • Cover with the lid and infuse for 15-20 minutes. 
  • Using a fine mesh strainer, strain the infusion, pressing the flower with the back of a spoon to release the liquid. Reserve the liquid and compost the flowers.
  • Add the aloe vera gel to the bottle.
  • Add the geranium essential oil and vitamin E, if using. 
  • Top the bottle off with your lavender water, leaving room to insert the spray top. 
  • Cap, shake gently, and label the bottle. 
  • Keep the mixture refrigerated until ready to use. 
  • To use, mist it on your skin after prolonged sun exposure.

Safety Note

  • If using a thin aloe vera gel,  combine essential oil with ½-1 teaspoon of carrier oil, such as sweet almond oil or olive oil, prior to combining with other ingredients to help safely disperse the essential oil. To help disperse the essential oils evenly, be sure to shake the bottle before each use. 
  • Store in the refrigerator and use within 1 week. 

St. John’s Wort Infused oil 

Blooming around the summer solstice, St. John’s wort and summer go hand in hand. Many folks know about its ability to ease mild to moderate and seasonal affective disorder (SAD), however herbalists also love this herb for its topical uses. The flowers can be utilized topically to soothe sore muscles, ease nerve discomfort as well as mild pain and inflammation associated with sciatica, and address viral skin issues like cold sores and shingles.

While dried herbs are generally used for making herb-infused oils, St. John’s wort flowers are an exception to this rule. The fresh flower buds hold a remarkable secret: when squeezed, they release a deep red substance rich in active constituents. This color is derived from the plant’s vibrant red pigment, hypericin. Infusing fresh St. John’s wort buds and flowers into oil captures this vivid red hue and extracts their beneficial qualities.

Ingredients

Fresh St. John’s wort (Hypericum perforatum) aerial parts
Carrier oil of your choice (olive oil, jojoba oil, sweet almond oil, fractionated coconut oil, etc.)

Directions

  • Pick fresh St. John’s wort buds and flowers and let them wilt for 24 hours to reduce moisture content a bit. Then chop them finely, and place in a  glass canning jar, filling the jar ¾ of the way.
  • Cover buds and flowers with organic extra-virgin olive oil, covering the herbs by 1 inch. Cap, and label with the date and ingredients. Alternatively, you can cover it with cheesecloth or a mesh top to allow the evaporation of water during the infusion process.
  • Place the jar in a brown paper bag and leave the mixture on a sunny windowsill for 4-6 weeks. Check intermittently to ensure moisture is not condensing in the jar; if this is the case, replace cap with a mesh top or several layers of cheesecloth so it can escape. 
  • When the oil is deep red, strain and bottle your final product into a labeled glass bottle.
  • Store the bottled oil in a cool, dark location.

Uses
St. John’s Wort oil is a wonderful summer herbal ally! Here are some summer-friendly uses to explore:

  • After-Sun Soothing Oil – St. John’s wort oil is commonly used to ease the skin after sun exposure. It supports skin that’s feeling warm, dry, or weather-worn. Simply apply a small amount to the skin after being in the sun to cool and calm.
  • Bug Bite or Sting Soother – Its comforting qualities make it perfect for calming irritated skin after insect bites or minor stings. Dab a bit of the oil directly onto the bite or sting to ease the discomfort.
  • Herbal Body Oil – Use it as an everyday summer body oil, especially for dry elbows, knees, or legs that have been out in the sun. Mix with calendula-infused oil for extra skin-loving goodness.
  • Bath Oil Blend – Add a tablespoon of St. John’s wort oil to a warm bath for a nourishing soak.
  • Mix with a little Epsom salt or milk first to help it disperse in the water.
  • Massage Oil for Neck and Shoulders – Perfect for tension held in the neck, shoulders, or lower back—especially when we’re more active in summer months. Try it with a few drops of lavender or peppermint essential oil, if desired. 
  • Salve or Balm – Combine St. John’s wort oil with beeswax to create a solid balm you can toss in your bag for on-the-go skin support. It’s great for hiking kits, beach bags, or camping trips.

Safety Note
While topical application is generally considered safe, photosensitization is possible, particularly in fair-skinned individuals or when used before sun exposure. For this reason, it’s best to apply St. John’s wort after sun exposure, not before spending time outdoors.

Although absorption through the skin is typically minimal, it can vary depending on the area of application, frequency of use, and whether the skin is broken or sensitive. Because of this, those taking medications with known interactions (like antidepressants, anticoagulants, or other drugs with a narrow therapeutic window) should consult with a healthcare provider before frequent or long-term topical use.

After-Sun Cream

This decadent, anti-inflammatory, and antioxidant cream is lovely any time of year, but is particularly beneficial for sun-exposed skin.

Ingredients

6 tablespoons (3 fl oz) St John’s wort infused oil (see recipe above)
2 tablespoons rose (Rosa spp.) petal
½ cup distilled water
2 tablespoon mango butter
½ tablespoon beeswax
¼ cup aloe (Aloe vera) leaf gel
Natural preservative (optional)

Directions

  • Make the St. John’s wort infused oil if you haven’t already (see recipe above). Use 6 tablespoons of finished oil in the recipe. (You may need to make more than 6 tablespoons initially, as herbs will absorb some oil.)
  • To prepare the rose-infusion, place rose petals in a heat-safe vessel and boil water. Pour freshly boiled water over rose petals. Cover and steep for 15 minutes, and strain. You’ll need ¼ cup of the infusion for this recipe.
  • Place infused oil, mango butter, and beeswax in a double boiler over low heat. Heat until melted, stirring occasionally, until fully combined. 
  • Remove from heat and let cool to room temperature or until the edges just begin to harden.
  • In a blender, combine ¼ cup rose infusion and aloe vera gel. If using a natural preservative that needs to go in the water phase, add it now and stir well; otherwise, wait until after the cream has emulsified. 
  • Blend on high, then slowly pour the cooled oil mixture into the blender. The emulsion should begin to thicken after about ⅔ of the oil is added. Continue slowly adding the rest until fully incorporated.
  • Blend until the cream is silky and smooth. Do not overmix.
  • Pour into sanitized glass jars. Label and refrigerate. Use within 1-2 weeks (longer if using a preservative).

Note

Feel free to adapt this recipe, using other herbs for the infused oil and/or rose infusion. Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) aerial parts, tulsi (Ocimum tenuiflorum) aerial parts, calendula (Calendula officinalis) flower, and plantain (Plantago spp.) leaf would all be great to include. 

POISON IVY SUPPORT

Jewelweed Poison Ivy Support Poultice 

A simple, plant-based preparation to keep on hand for outdoor skin support—especially helpful after brushing up against poison ivy but also useful after exposure.

Ingredients

Fresh jewelweed (Impatiens capensis, I. pallida) leaves and stems
Clean cloth or gauze (for application, optional)

Directions

To use fresh jewelweed immediately

  • Gather a handful of fresh jewelweed leaves and stems, ensuring they are not harvested near roadsides or chemically treated areas.
  • Using your hands, a stone, or a mortar and pestle, mash the plant material into a juicy pulp.
  • Place the crushed jewelweed directly onto the skin where you think you may have contacted poison ivy. You can also wrap it in clean cloth or gauze and hold it against the area.
  • Let it sit for 10-20 minutes, then gently rinse with cool water.

To prepare and store for future use

  • Use a juicer to extract the liquid from fresh jewelweed leaves and stems or puree them in a blender with a bit of water.
  • Pour the liquid into ice cube trays. Once frozen, transfer the cubes to a freezer bag or container.
  • Rub a frozen cube gently over the skin or thaw and apply with a cloth as a poultice.

ADDITIONAL SUPPORT FOR POISON IVY

Not everyone has access to jewelweed, especially if you’re far from wooded areas or wild streams where it tends to grow. Thankfully, there are plenty of other plant-based and pantry-friendly options that can offer comfort and support after contact with poison ivy. Below is a list of simple, accessible alternatives you can turn to when jewelweed isn’t available.

  • Plantain (Plantago spp.) Poultice – Chew a fresh leaf or mash it with a bit of water to create a moist paste. Apply directly to the skin for a soothing, drawing effect.
  • Aloe (Aloe vera) Leaf Gel – Use the fresh inner gel from the aloe leaf to cool and calm irritated skin. 
  • Calendula (Calendula officinalis) Compress – Make a strong tea of the fresh or dried blossoms and use as a skin rinse or compress. Calendula is known for its skin-soothing properties and gentle support for inflamed areas.
  • Colloidal or Ground Oats (Avena sativa) – Add finely ground oats to a lukewarm bath or try the Herbal Oat Bath recipe below. This offers gentle support for itchy or irritated skin.
  • Baking Soda – Add ½ cup to a bath for a simple soak.
  • Bentonite Clay – Combine with just enough water, hydrosol, or herbal tea to form a paste and apply as a mask over the area to help draw irritation out and calm the skin. Let the clay dry completely before rinsing off with cool water.
  • Hydrosol Spritz or Compress – Spray calendula (Calendula officinalis), lavender (Lavendula spp.), peppermint (Mentha x piperita), or yarrow (Achillea millefolium) hydrosol on the skin or soak a cloth in hydrosol and apply as a compress for comfort to help ease the itch.
  • Witch Hazel (Hamamelis virginiana) Hydrosol – Apply with a cotton pad as a gentle astringent to help dry out weepy areas and calm irritation.

Herbal Oat Bath for Dry, Itchy Skin

Oat is a well-loved ingredient when it comes to caring for dry, irritated skin. Combined with baking soda and calendula, this bath offers a soothing experience, perfect after a day in the sun or time spent outdoors. Try it in the evening to wind down before bed.

Ingredients

2 cups rolled oats (Avena sativa)
1/4 cup baking soda
1 cup calendula (Calendula officinalis) flowers 

Directions

  • For best results, pulse the oats in a blender or food processor until finely ground. This helps them disperse more effectively in the water.
  • Combine all ingredients in a muslin bag or a square of cheesecloth and tie securely.
  • Fill the tub with lukewarm water, dropping the bag in as it fills. Swish it around to help release the botanicals. The water may turn cloudy—this is a good thing.
  • Soak for 15-20 minutes, letting the herbal blend float and infuse the bath.
  • When finished, pat your skin dry with a soft towel.

Plantain, Calendula, and Aloe Succus Cubes

A succus is the freshly pressed juice of a plant, and this blend combines three skin-loving herbs—plantain (Plantago spp.), calendula (Calendula officinalis), and aloe (Aloe vera)—into a hydrating, cooling mixture ideal for topical use. Once juiced and stirred together, the succus can be frozen into cubes for quick use. These icy cubes are perfect for applying to sun-kissed skin, bug bites, or everyday scrapes, burns, rashes, and scratches.

Ingredients

1 part fresh plantain (Plantago spp.) leaf
1 part fresh calendula (Calendula officinalis) flower
1 part fresh aloe (Aloe vera) leaf

Note: Measure parts by volume.

Directions

  • Separate the soft inner flesh of the aloe from the tough exterior.
  • Run only the soft fleshy interior part of the aloe plant through your juicer or blender.
  • Use a juicer or blender to extract the juice from the aloe, plantain, and calendula. 
  • Once the juice is extracted, give it a good stir with a whisk or a fork. The plant juices will have varying viscosities and densities, and you’ll want to be sure they are well combined before moving on to the next step.
  • After you have given the succus a good stir, pour the mixture into ice trays and freeze. Once the cubes are frozen they can be placed in a storage container in the freezer until use. Use ice trays in different shapes to appeal to kids.

Note

Feel free to swap the herbs for other herbs that are well-suited to your situation. For example, a jewelweed (Impatiens capensis) succus would be a great choice for poison ivy.

Outdoor Essentials

Summer invites us outside—into gardens, onto trails, and around campfires. But with that outdoor fun often comes a few pesky companions like bugs, sunburns, stings, and scrapes. Whether you’re hiking, gardening, or just spending time in the backyard, it’s helpful to have a few herbal allies on hand to support comfort and ease in the moment.

Below you’ll find some simple, go-to herbal recipes that are perfect for your summer first-aid kit or picnic basket. From bug sprays and bite salves to sting poultices and cooling foot powders, these preparations can help you stay grounded, refreshed, and ready for more time in the sun.

Herbal Bug Spray for Clothing 

Bugs are a natural part of outdoor life, but they don’t have to ruin your summer fun. This herbal spray combines aromatic plants traditionally used to help discourage insects while being gentle on the skin and pleasant to the senses. Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) and lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) form the foundation of this blend, while essential oils adds a powerful, plant-based punch. This recipe is a great natural option to keep in your summer bag.

Ingredients

Handful fresh yarrow (Achillea millefolium) aerial parts
Handful fresh lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) aerial parts
Vodka or witch hazel hydrosol
¼ cup distilled water 
24 drops catnip (Nepeta cataria) essential oil, optional 
24 drops rose geranium (Pelargonium graveolens) essential oil, optional 

Directions

  • Fill a clean pint-sized glass jar about halfway with fresh yarrow and lemon balm.
  • Pour vodka or witch hazel hydrosol over the herbs until they are fully covered.
  • Seal the jar with a tight-fitting lid and store it in a cool, dark place for about 2 weeks. Give it a gentle shake every few days.
  • After 2 weeks, strain the mixture using a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth, reserving the liquid and composting the herbs.
  • To make the spray, combine ¼ cup of the infused liquid with ¼ cup of distilled water in a ½ cup (4 fl oz) spray bottle.
  • Add essential oil, if using.
  • Cap the bottle and store in the fridge. Shake well before each use.

Note

  • If adding essential oil, spray the blend on clothing only and not directly on the skin, since the essential oils are not diluted in a carrier oil. 
  • Feel free to add a handful of fresh catnip in the first step if you have any on hand. 

Bee Sting Poultice

Bee stings are never fun, but a quick herbal poultice can offer natural comfort right when it’s needed most. Lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) and plantain (Plantago spp.) are two common, easy-to-find herbs traditionally used to soothe irritated skin. This simple preparation can be made in the moment, whether you’re at home or out on a hike, using just a few fresh leaves and either a splash of water or vinegar or even your own spit. It’s a time-tested, no-fuss way to support the skin after a sting.

Ingredients

A couple of leaves of fresh lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) or plantain (Plantago spp.)
A splash of water, apple cider vinegar, or spit

Directions

  • Before making your poultice, properly remove the bee’s stinger.
  • Harvest a couple of leaves of fresh lemon balm or plantain.
  • If using water or apple cider vinegar, smash and roll the leaves together in your fingers or hands to release some of the volatile oils or juices. Place them in a small bowl and mix the herbs with a splash of water or apple cider vinegar to make a poultice. If using spit, put the leaves in your mouth and chew them up well to make a spit-based poultice.
  • Take the poultice and apply directly onto the sting site. 
  • Wrap a snug-fitting bandage (or 1-2 whole plantain leaves) over the top of the poultice to hold it in place. Leave the wrap on for about 30 minutes then remove.
  • If the swelling is persistent and the stung area still painful, apply a new poultice, repeating as often as needed.

Itch-Soother Salve

Bug bites and other itchy summer irritations are no match for this creamy, plant-powered salve. With skin-loving herbs like plantain, cooling peppermint, calming lavender, and the soothing texture of shea butter, this blend offers a gentle way to ease discomfort and nourish irritated skin. Bentonite clay is a key ingredient here—it’s known for its ability to draw out impurities and calm inflamed or itchy spots, adding an extra layer of comfort to this summertime staple.

Ingredients

½ cup plantain (Plantago spp.) infused olive oil
1 tablespoon beeswax
1 tablespoon shea butter
2 teaspoons bentonite clay
5 drops peppermint (Mentha x piperita) essential oil 
5 drops lavender (Lavandula angustifolia) essential oil

Directions

  • Combine the plantain-infused oil, beeswax, and shea butter in a double boiler over low heat (or in a heat-safe bowl or jar set over a saucepan of gently simmering water).
  • Stir occasionally until the beeswax and shea butter are fully melted and the mixture is thoroughly combined.
  • Whisk in the bentonite clay until well combined.
  • Remove from heat and add the peppermint and lavender essential oil. (Skip the peppermint essential oil if using for kids under age of 6 years). 
  • Pour the mixture into clean glass jars and allow to cool. You will see the liquid solidify and become lighter in color.
  • The end texture is creamy and brings a soothing ease to itchy skin.
  • Once fully cooled, cover with lids and label.
  • Stored in a cool, dark place, this salve will keep for about 6 months.

Cuts and Scrapes Salve

This simple salve is a must-have for any first-aid kit. Made with calendula, yarrow, and plantain, it’s designed to gently support the skin through life’s little bumps and scrapes. Whether you’re dealing with a minor cut, scrape, or patch of dry skin, this salve offers soothing, herbal comfort in a convenient, easy-to-use form. 

Ingredients

For the infused oil

3 tablespoons calendula (Calendula officinalis) flower
3 tablespoons yarrow (Achillea millefolium) aerial parts
2 tablespoons plantain (Plantago spp.) leaf
1 cup olive oil

For the salve

½ cup infused herbal oil
1-2 tablespoons beeswax pellets

Directions

  • Combine the calendula, yarrow, and plantain with the olive oil in a sanitized glass jar.
  • Cover tightly and shake gently to mix. 
  • Let infuse in a cool, dark place for 4-6 weeks, shaking occasionally.
  • When ready to make the salve, strain the herbs from the oil using a fine mesh strainer lined with cheesecloth.
  • Measure out ½ cup of the infused oil.
  • Place infused oil and beeswax in a double boiler over low heat (or a glass or ceramic bowl or canning jar over a small saucepan of simmering water) until beeswax has melted and ingredients are thoroughly combined.
  • Dip a clean spoon into the formula and place it into a refrigerator so the salve will cool quickly and you can determine if its consistency is what you desire. If the sample is too soft, add a little more wax to the warm salve; if the sample is too hard, add a little more of the infused oil.
  • While the mixture is warm, pour individual portions into appropriate containers and allow to cool at room temperature. 
  • Once cool, put the lids on and label. 
  • Store in a cool, dark place for up to 1 year.

First Aid Kit Yarrow Tincture

While yarrow is often found in fields and roadsides, it’s always wise to come prepared with a small bottle of yarrow tincture in case you can’t find it growing fresh where you are. A yarrow tincture can help cleanse wounds, stop bleeding, soothe rashes and intestinal distress, lower fevers, and more. 

Ingredients

1 ounce (by weight) yarrow (Achillea millefolium) aerial parts (if using fresh yarrow, wilt for 24 hours)
5 fluid ounces (by volume) of vodka or brandy (80 proof or higher)

Directions

  • Fill a clean, sanitized jar with weighed dried yarrow. Pour vodka or brandy over the herbs until they are completely covered. Dry herbs may absorb the liquid, so check back and add more alcohol if needed.
  • Set in a dark place to steep for 4-6 weeks, shaking every few days. 
  • Strain tincture through a fine mesh strainer or several layers of cheesecloth into a clean jar, pressing on herb material to release every last drop of tincture. Compost the yarrow. 
  • Transfer tincture to a clean, amber bottle with a dropper cap for use and label. Store in a cool, dark place. 

To use

Squirt a small amount of yarrow tincture in warm water to cleanse wounds and slow bleeding or to apply to rashes. Add 10-20 drops to a cup of hot water to support digestion and fevers.

Cooling Herbal Foot Powder 

Keep your feet fresh and comfortable all summer long with this cooling herbal foot powder. Made with summer botanicals and skin-loving ingredients, it helps absorb moisture, reduce odor, and support tired, overheated feet—perfect after a long day in the heat or on your feet.

Ingredients

½ cup arrowroot powder 
¼ cup white kaolin clay
¼ cup baking soda
1 tablespoon peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf, finely ground 
1 tablespoon rose (Rosa spp.) petal, finely ground 
½ teaspoon activated charcoal, optional 
½ teaspoon jojoba oil or fractionated coconut oil
5 drops peppermint (Mentha x piperita) essential oil 
5 drops tea tree (Melaleuca alternifolia) essential oil 
5 drops lavender (Lavandula angustifolia) essential oil 

Directions

  • In a mixing bowl, whisk together the arrowroot powder, kaolin clay, baking soda, and activated charcoal (if using).
  • Use a spice grinder or mortar and pestle to finely grind the peppermint and rose. Add them to the bowl and stir well.
  • Place jojoba or coconut oil in a small cup and add essential oils. Stir to combine.
  • Mix essential oil blend into the powder, whisking to distribute evenly.
  • If powder has clumps after adding the oil blend, sift through a fine mesh strainer.
  • Store in a shaker bottle, jar with a fine strainer lid, or an airtight container.
  • To use, sprinkle a small amount onto clean, dry feet or into shoes.

Note

  • For extra cooling, store the powder in the fridge before use.
  • For a stronger antifungal blend, add a teaspoon of ground calendula or neem powder.
  • For a milder scent, reduce the essential oils or use only lavender.

Conclusion

Summer herbs offer so many simple and beautiful ways to support the body through the warmth, activity, and occasional discomforts of the season. Whether you’re sipping a cooling tea, misting your skin with a refreshing spray, or pulling a soothing salve from your bag after a day outdoors, these preparations help bring ease, comfort, and connection to the natural world around you.

And the best part of all? Many of these herbs are easy to grow, find, or forage—and the recipes themselves are flexible, allowing you to adapt based on what you have on hand. With just a few thoughtful preparations, you can create a seasonal toolkit that helps you move through summer feeling more supported, balanced, and in rhythm with the earth.
