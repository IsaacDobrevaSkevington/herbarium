Rosemary

60 minutes

Introduction

Rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus), a Mediterranean evergreen mint with aromatic pine needle-like branches, is a delightful herb to use during the holidays. This warming herb helps to boost the spirit and is often used to help stimulate circulation to the brain, aiding memory, focus, and even easing headaches. It has antimicrobial actions that are used by herbalists to soothe colds and the flu as well as infections of the respiratory tract. Rosemary also benefits digestion with its mildly bitter flavor and carminative properties.

Rosemary’s species name, rosmarinus, means “dew of the sea” and refers to the sea spray that accumulates on plants that grow by the sea. Traditionally, rosemary was also linked to the feminine, associated with the Virgin Mary, and used by Greeks to adorn young women. 

Despite favoring warm climates, rosemary’s dark green needle-like leaves resemble evergreens that are emblematic of winter and the holiday season. In the same fashion as other mint family plants, rosemary has a square stem; it becomes dark gray, scaly, and woody as it grows. Flowering occurs May to July in warm climates, and later or not at all in colder climates. Blooms are small pale blue, pink, or white flowers arranged in whorls around the stem.

Rosemary has long been associated with improvements in memory and fondly referred to as “the herb of remembrance.” As a symbol and reminder of true friendship and lovers’ fidelity, rosemary has traditions in ceremonies associated with marriage, love, and death—an ideal herb during a time of the year when there is a focus on family, traditions, memories, and possibly feelings of nostalgia. 

Rosemary’s taste can be described as pungent, and slightly bitter, corresponding to its warming, drying, stimulating, and restorative energetics (Holmes, 1997). Rosemary has an affinity for the nervous system, the cardiovascular system, and the digestive system.

Rosemary’s warming and stimulating energetic properties help to increase blood flow to the brain, explaining its strong use as a focus and memory-enhancing brain tonic. Due to this same warming and stimulating action, Herbalist Judith Berger (1998) credits rosemary for “dispelling sluggishness in both body and spirit” and it is therefore useful for anxiety, depression, insomnia, lethargy, nervousness, fatigue, exhaustion, stress, headaches, and migraines. In the cardiovascular system, rosemary has both restorative and stimulating actions, acting as a cardiotonic and warming arterial stimulant (Holmes, 1997).

Rosemary is considered an uplifting digestive tonic with antispasmodic, cholagogue, and carminative actions. It relaxes and tones the stomach due to its nervine actions, making it especially helpful in cases of digestive upset caused by mental tension, anxiety, and worry. As a cholagogue, rosemary stimulates digestion and detoxification by increasing the production and flow of bile from the liver. The volatile oils in rosemary are largely responsible for its carminative action, helping to ease flatulence and indigestion. 

The volatile oils in rosemary also contribute to its antimicrobial and diaphoretic properties, making rosemary an important ally for colds, sore throats, the flu, coughs, and clearing congestion when infection does take hold.

Rosemary has much to offer for culinary and wellness uses, and this verdant and sturdy plant is a beautiful addition to herbal crafts, decorative projects, and gifts. 

Safety: Note that only culinary amounts of rosemary should be used during pregnancy.

Culinary Uses

Rosemary adds a distinctive, aroma and flavor to both sweet and savory dishes, making it a must-have in the kitchen during the holiday season. Its woodsy, herbaceous flavor compliments everything from baked goods and savory breads to roasted vegetables and meats. Whether you’re infusing olive oil with rosemary for drizzling over dishes or baking delicate herbal shortbread, this versatile herb can elevate simple recipes into something special. Below, you’ll find a few delicious recipes to try, including rosemary-infused olive oil, rosemary shortbread, and a flavorful rosemary seasoning salt.

Rosemary & Honey Shortbread 

Signifying remembrance, rosemary brings a sense of continuity to a time of year when many folks are looking back on the past year and taking stock for the new one to follow. And if you love all things rosemary and butter, then these melt-in-your-mouth shortbread cookies are the perfect cookie for you! They are not your typical holiday cookie, offering a rich buttery flavor complemented with bright herbaceousness. The rosemary really is delightful, making this a wonderful addition to any cookie platter.

Ingredients

2 sticks salted butter or non-dairy butter, room temperature
¼ cup honey
2 cups unbleached white flour or cup-for-cup gluten-free blend
1 tablespoon lemon or orange zest 
1 tablespoon fresh rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus), minced
Optional add-ins: a pinch of dried lavender (Lavendula spp.) flower buds, walnuts, chocolate chips, or jam (for making thumbprint cookies). Tasty flavors to consider for thumbprint option: fruit jam or orange marmalade (add after baking)

Directions

      • In a large mixing bowl, cream the butter until fluffy.
      • Add honey and beat to combine well.
      • Add the flour, lemon zest, rosemary, and any optional add-ins, continuing to beat until all the ingredients are blended together well.
      • Preheat the oven to 325 degrees F.

For plain shortbread cookies:

      • Chill the dough for at least 30 minutes.
      • Roll the dough into 1 inch balls and place on a baking cookie sheet.
      • Press the dough down a bit with a cookie press, fork, or your fingers. Poke a hole (or holes) all the way through the shortbread.
      • Bake for 15 to 20 minutes or until the edges are slightly brown.

For thumbprint cookies:

      • Chill the dough for at least 30 minutes.
      • Roll the dough into 1 inch balls and place on a greased baking sheet.
      • Press a thumbprint into the center of each ball. Add a filling such as jam or chocolate chips.
      • Bake for 15 to 20 minutes or until the edges are slightly brown.

Note that the cookies may be very crumbly until they cool and harden.

Rosemary-Infused Olive Oil – Two Ways

Rosemary-infused olive oil has a robust, earthy taste that is a wonderful recipe enhancer. The infusion brings out the aromatic notes from the rosemary, creating a rich and distinctive flavor. Drizzle this herbaceous oil over salads, vegetables, and meats, or pour it into a small bowl and use as a dipping sauce that will make any meal stand out. Below are two methods of making this delicious infused oil using dried or fresh rosemary, depending on the preparation time and shelf life you prefer.

Rosemary-Infused Olive Oil

For the longest shelf-life and to avoid the possibility for growth of the botulism-causing bacterium (Clostridium botulinum), it is best to make this oil using dried herbs. If you need rosemary oil in a hurry, see the second recipe below for a quick version using fresh rosemary, with some important safety caveats.

Ingredients

Rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf
Olive (Olea europaea) oil

Directions

      • Start with a clean, dry, sanitized glass jar.
      • Fill jar ½ full with dried rosemary and pour room-temperature oil over the herb, filling the jar with oil.
      • Cap tightly and place the jar in a cool, dark place for 1-2 weeks. Alternatively, the oil can be infused via a heated infusion. To do this, preheat the oil to 180 degrees F, then pour over the herbs in the jar. Cap and let infuse for 1-2 weeks.
      • Shake the bottle gently every few days to help distribute the flavors. 
      • Once infused, strain the oil after the infusion period using a fine mesh strainer or cheesecloth to remove the rosemary. 
      • Transfer the strained oil back into a clean bottle or jar. 
      • Once strained, this oil is best kept in the refrigerator and used within a few weeks. A new sprig of dried herb or chili pepper can be added for presentation.

Safety note

Be mindful of storage; homemade infused culinary oils should typically be used within a few weeks to prevent spoilage. If you notice any off smells or changes in appearance, discard the oil.

Quick Rosemary-Infused Olive Oil

If you’re hosting a holiday party and want to make something unique and delicious for your guests, here’s an easy and quick herbal trick. Creating a flavorful infused oil on short notice is the perfect job for rosemary!

Ingredients

2-3 sprigs fresh rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf
1-2 cups olive (Olea europaea) oil

Directions

      • Prepare the rosemary by rinsing it under cold water and patting it dry to remove as much water as possible. 
      • Place rosemary in the glass bottle or jar and pour the olive oil over it, ensuring the rosemary is fully submerged. 
      • Seal and store the bottle or jar tightly. The rosemary will get to work right away, adding a subtle flavor to your oil just in time for dinner. 
      • For a stronger flavor, place rosemary and olive oil in a saucepan and warm gently over medium-low heat, keeping the temperature below 180°F. Allow the rosemary to infuse in the warm oil for 10-15 minutes, watching to ensure it doesn’t overheat. Remove from the heat, letting the oil cool completely before straining into a clean glass jar. This oil is best used within 1-2 days. For a beautiful party presentation, add a fresh sprig of rosemary to the bottle. 
      • Hosting the next day? Seal and store the bottle or jar tightly, and place it in the refrigerator. 

To Use

      • The infused oil can be used for cooking, drizzling, or as a dip.

Safety notes

      • Infused oils must be prepared carefully to ensure that they do not become contaminated with the botulism-causing organism (Clostridium botulinum), a potentially deadly microorganism which can be found in soil particles on fresh herbs.
      • Homemade infused oils using fresh herbs should be stored in the fridge and used within 1-2 days.

Rosemary Seasoning Salt Blends

Elevate your dishes with these two flavorful rosemary seasoning salt blends! Each one is simple to prepare and offers a unique twist that enhances a variety of meals. These homemade blends are not only delicious but also make for thoughtful gifts.

Rosemary Garlic Salt Blend

This homemade rosemary-garlic salt blend is quick to prepare and much tastier than store-bought seasoning blends! It’s ideal for seasoning steaks, roasts, chicken, vegetables, soaps, stews, and more. A great staple for your own kitchen, this blend also makes a delicious and unique gift. Use dried rosemary to create a blend that will last long term. 

Yield: Approximately ½ cup

Ingredients

4 tbsp sea salt
2 tbsp garlic (Allium sativum) clove, powdered
1 tbsp rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf, crumbled or powdered
1 tsp black pepper (Piper nigrum) fruit, powdered

Directions

      • Place all ingredients in a small bowl and stir until well blended. 
      • Transfer to a sanitized glass jar with a lid.
      • Label, and store in a cool, dark place.
      • Use within 6 months.

To Use

      • Use as you would any seasoning salt, sprinkling over meats and vegetables and adding to soups and stews. 

Rosemary Nettle Salt Blend

his rosemary nettle salt blend is a flavorful and nutritious seasoning that combines the aromatic notes of rosemary with the nutritive benefits of nettle and alfalfa. This blend enhances a variety of dishes, from roasted vegetables to grilled meats. 

Yield: Approximately ½ cup

Ingredients

2 tbsp rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf
1 tbsp nettle (Urtica dioica) leaf 
1 tbsp alfalfa (Medicago sativa) aerial parts
1 tbsp thyme (Thymus vulgaris) aerial parts
3 tbsp sea salt

Directions

      • Place herbs in a bowl and stir until well blended.
      • Add the sea salt and stir to mix evenly with the herbs. 
      • If you prefer a finer texture, transfer the mixture to a spice grinder and grind until you achieve your desired consistency. 
      • Transfer to a sanitized glass jar with a lid.
      • Label, and store in a cool, dark place.
      • Use within 6 months.

To Use

      • Sprinkle on roasted vegetables, meats, or fish for added flavor.
      • Use to season soups, stews, or sauces.
      • Package in a decorative jar for a thoughtful homemade gift.

Wellness Uses

In addition to its culinary uses, rosemary has long been treasured for its health benefits. In this section, you’ll find simple recipes to harness rosemary’s wellness potential—a revitalizing hair rinse to promote shine and scalp health, and a memory-boosting elixir to keep your mind sharp and focused. And don’t forget your tea cup where rosemary stars as a delicious revitalizing tea: to make, simply add 1-2 teaspoons of rosemary leaves per cup of hot water and steep for 10 minutes.

Rosemary for Haircare

Whether it be sun, wind, or humidity, nature often provides trying conditions for hair! During this late fall and early winter season, we may experience dry, brittle hair, frizz and static, or lack of luster and shine. Scalp concerns, such as dandruff and dryness, are also common.

If your hair is feeling dull or damaged, here’s your invitation to indulge in some herbal hair care! 

Rosemary is a powerhouse herb for hair health, offering nourishing benefits for all hair types. Known for its invigorating and strengthening properties, rosemary can breathe life back into lackluster locks. From supporting scalp health to promoting shine, rosemary is a versatile herb that can address multiple hair needs.

Rosemary’s hair-supportive benefits:

  • For oily hair: Rosemary helps regulate oil production and leaves your scalp feeling refreshed. Pair with lavender (Lavandula spp.) bud or lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) aerial parts for additional benefits. 
  • To strengthen hair: Known for its fortifying properties, rosemary can strengthen weak or thinning hair. Horsetail (Equisetum spp.) leaf and nettle (Urtica dioica) leaf are great additions to this blend. 
  • To add shine: Rosemary is often used to enhance shine and bring out the natural beauty of your hair. Pair with oat (Avena sativa) straw for more shiny hair support.
  • To invigorate the scalp: With its stimulating nature, rosemary promotes circulation, which can support healthy hair growth. Pair with peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf, if desired. 
  • To enhance dark hair: Rosemary deepens dark tones, making it a favorite for those with darker hair colors. Sage (Salvia officinalis) aerial parts are also helpful in this endeavor. 

Rosemary Hair Rinse

One of the easiest ways to incorporate rosemary into your routine is an herbal vinegar rinse, which provides many benefits such as softening hair, removing dirt and residue, adding shine, and invigorating the scalp. Raw apple cider vinegar is a boon to encourage healthy locks. It may help restore your hair’s pH balance and ease a range of concerns, from oily hair to an itchy, dry scalp, dandruff, and even some dermatitis and fungal issues. 

This vinegar rinse can be created as a single application for a special at-home spa day, but may also be incorporated into your weekly hair routine. If you plan to use it on a regular basis, once or twice a week is plenty, as more than that may be too drying. Note that the vinegar scent is very faint to nonexistent once your hair is dry.

Ingredients

2 cups apple cider vinegar
½ cup dried rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf or 1 cup fresh

Directions

      • Place jar on a sturdy surface and add the herbs.
      • Pour apple cider vinegar over the herbs until they are well covered and vinegar is at least an inch above the top of the herbs. 
      • Close tightly with lid and shake jar to ensure the vinegar and herbs are thoroughly mixed.
      • Set jar in a cool, dark place and let it sit for at least a week (2-4 weeks is optimal). Shake jar every few days.
      • Strain herbs from vinegar using a fine mesh strainer lined with cheesecloth. Gather ends of cheesecloth and squeeze herbs with your hands to release every last drop of vinegar. 
      • Bottle the vinegar and don’t forget to add a label.
      • Store in a cool, dark place for up to 6 months. 

​To Use

      • A vinegar rinse may be used after shampoo in place of conditioner (a couple times a week) or as a stand-alone application. 
      • When ready to use, pour 2-3 tablespoons of vinegar into a plastic container (avoid glass jars in the shower due to breakage concerns). At this point you can choose to either use the vinegar rinse at full strength (best for oily hair) or dilute it with water (best for dry hair). You can experiment with different ratios of vinegar to water to find the best balance for you. 
      • ​Starting at the scalp, pour vinegar rinse over damp hair when you are ready to get out of the bath or shower, as there is no need to rinse out the vinegar. Work thoroughly into hair with fingers. It is helpful to massage the scalp to ensure all the herbal goodness reaches the roots and supports any scalp concerns.

Notes

      • Use fresh or dried herbs. If using fresh, let herbs wilt a day or two to reduce moisture content before making herbal vinegar.
      • Since vinegar will corrode metal, use a plastic lid if possible. If you use a metal lid, place a piece of wax paper between the jar and lid before sealing.
      • Do not mix water with the infused herbal vinegar until ready to use in order to avoid any potential bacterial growth.
      • Plastic bottles with a “squirt” spout work very well for application.
      • Depending on the herbs used, your herbal vinegar may double as a facial toner!

Rosemary for Remembrance

The physical workings that make our memory what it is are complex, but when it comes to physical health beyond diet and lifestyle, supporting memory health with herbs focuses on keeping the cardiovascular, nervous, and endocrine systems healthy and strong, as these are the systems that affect memory. As a circulatory stimulant, rosemary not only increases blood flow and tones the vascular system, but it stimulates the nervous system and has been shown in various studies to positively affect memory.

Rosemary Memory Elixir

The following elixir can be used daily to support memory health. It’s made using dried herbs in order to store the mix long-term, but you can certainly make it with fresh herbs if you wish! Precautions: If you have heart disease or are on cardiac medications, consult with your doctor before using. If you are pregnant, consult with your obstetrician or midwife before using.

Ingredients

4 parts rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf
2 parts ginkgo (Ginkgo biloba) leaf
2 parts oat (Avena sativa) straw
1 part gotu kola (Centella asiatica) leaf
1 part peppermint (Mentha x piperita) leaf
Brandy
Raw honey

Directions

      • Combine dried herbs and mix well. Label and store the mixture in a cool, dark place until you’re ready to make your elixir.
      • When you’re ready to make your rosemary memory elixir, fill a sanitized, dry pint-sized glass pint jar half full of the herb blend.
      • Fill the jar half full with brandy, and then fill the remaining half of the jar with honey. Use a sanitized spoon to mix the herbs, brandy, and honey well.
      • Cover with a lid, label the jar, and place it in a cool, dark place for 6 weeks. Stir the mixture a few times a week, if you can remember.
      • After 6 weeks, strain the elixir and compost or discard the herbs. 
      • Bottle the elixir, label, and store in a cool, dark place. 

To use

Adults can take 30 drops of elixir daily.

Decorative Uses

Rosemary’s beauty makes it a perfect choice for natural decorations. Whether you’re crafting a fresh rosemary wreath or adding a touch of greenery to your holiday table, rosemary brings a fragrant, rustic charm to your home. Its evergreen leaves symbolize remembrance and enduring love, making it ideal for festive settings. Plus, the fragrance is sure to elevate the mood.

 

Fresh Rosemary Wreath

Enjoy the beautiful simplicity of a wreath made with rosemary, or fill out your wreath with a variety of fresh herbs, dried seed pods, branches of persistent berries and fruits, and even fresh or dried flowers. Look for evergreen branches of pine (Pinus spp.), juniper (Juniperus spp.), and holly (Ilex spp.). Other fragrant herbs to consider are sage (Salvia officinalis), thyme (Thymus vulgaris), lavender (Lavandula spp.), bay (Laurus nobilis), and eucalyptus (Eucalyptus spp.). You can even include stems of fresh roses (Rosa spp.)! Any flower that dries well or branches with persistent fruits such as rose (Rosa spp.) hips, hawthorn (Crataegus spp.) berry, and juniper (Juniperus spp.) berry (which are actually immature cones!) also make a lovely addition to fresh rosemary wreaths.

Materials

Fresh rosemary sprigs
Any additional fresh and dried botanicals you wish to use
Metal or grapevine wreath base
Florist wire
Snips or scissors
Pretty ribbon for hanging
Gardening gloves, optional for protecting hands from spiky plants

Directions

      • Start creating your fresh rosemary wreath by clustering small bunches of rosemary together into bundles. If you are using other plants along with the rosemary you can either bundle them separately or add them to your rosemary bundles. Make your bundles about 6 to 10 inches long depending on the size of your wreath with the largest sprigs in the back and the smallest sprigs towards the front. Secure each bundle at the base with florist wire and set aside.
      • Layer the bundles one on top of the other all the way around the wreath. Start by inserting the first bundle into the grapevine base. If you wish, you can use floral wire to attach the bundle to the grapevine. Insert the next bundle into the wreath base so that the top part of the second bundle covers the base of the first bundle. Do this all the way around your wreath until it is full and beautiful.
      • Finish up your fresh rosemary wreath by positioning any remaining flowers, seed pods, or berries in a pleasing way on your wreath. Use floral wire to attach. Then add a beautiful ribbon for hanging, and you are done!

Rosemary-Inspired Table Settings

A table runner full of fresh greenery, tapered candles, and other ornaments of holiday cheer can offer a stunning display that your family and guests will enjoy at mealtimes. Consider using pine (Pinus spp.), juniper (Juniperus spp.), cypress (Cupressus spp.), and magnolia (Magnolia spp.) as the greenery options for table centerpieces. Start by gathering several branches of greenery. Place the larger pieces along the table and add in smaller pieces as you go. Space tapered candles, herbs, or other ornaments throughout the greenery for a final touch.

Rosemary and Twine Silverware Wrap

Wrap napkins around silverware and secure them with twine or a small ribbon. Tuck a spring of rosemary under the twine/ribbon. Or, experiment with wrapping the rosemary around the napkin to create a napkin ring. It adds a natural, rustic touch to each place setting and brings a lovely aroma to the table.

Rosemary Place Cards

Attach small sprigs of rosemary to personalized place cards by punching two holes in the top of the card and threading a piece of rosemary through the holes. You can also use a small clothespin to clip a sprig of rosemary to the card. Another simple option is to tie a spice of rosemary to a large gift tag and tie around napkin or set next to a plate or glass as a place card.

Rosemary Mini-Wreath Place Cards

To create a rosemary mini wreath place card setting, start by trimming sprigs of rosemary to about 7-9 inches in length. Shape each sprig into a circle, softly rolling it between your fingers to loosen the stem and make it easier to bend. Secure the ends with floral wire, and add a small tag with the guest’s name. If any parts of the rosemary are sticking out, use extra bits of floral wire to tuck them into place. Finish with a small ribbon or twine for a festive accent.

Rosemary Centerpiece

Create a simple, minimalist centerpiece by filling small vases or jars with rosemary sprigs. Mix with other herbs or greenery, or pair with battery-powered candles for a soft, natural look. You could also place a few sprigs of rosemary directly on the table runner or tablecloth.

Gift Giving

Rosemary is more than just a flavorful herb—it’s a symbol of remembrance, love, and renewal, making it a perfect addition to handmade gifts. Its invigorating aroma adds a touch of warmth and comfort, while its natural benefits promote both beauty and wellness. Whether you’re crafting a soothing rosemary hair oil or blending a fragrant rosemary tea, these gifts offer a thoughtful and personal way to share the joy of natural living. Each creation is infused with the essence of rosemary, bringing both practicality and heartfelt meaning to your holiday gifting.

Smoothing Rosemary Hair Serum

This DIY herbal hair serum recipe calls for rosemary essential oil to help strengthen hair and add a calming yet stimulating, aromatic element. Rosemary is well-known to help stimulate hair growth when massaged into the scalp, but when applied to the rest of our locks, it can also assist in strengthening hair to prevent further breakage (Gladstar, 2001). All herbs in this recipe are dried. As this includes rosemary essential oil, it’s not for use with children under the age of 3 (some aromatherapists suggest avoiding in children under the age of 6).

Ingredients

½-1 tbsp gotu kola (Centella asiatica) leaf
½-1 tbsp calendula (Calendula officinalis) flower
3 tbsp jojoba oil 
25 drops vitamin E oil
10-15 drops rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) essential oil
1 tbsp argan oil

Directions

      • Place dried gotu kola and calendula in a heatproof glass jar and then cover with jojoba oil until the oil completely covers the herb. Cover jar with a lid.
      • Place a few jar lids or a washcloth in the bottom of a crockpot (this is a safety measure so that your glass jar does not come into direct contact with the heat source), then place the jar on top fill the crockpot with water until it reaches at least halfway up the side of the jar. (You may need to put a small weight, such as a rock, on top of the jar to keep it from floating around).
      • Turn the crockpot to the low setting and allow the herbs to infuse in the oil 4-8 hours, maintaining the temperature between 120-140 degrees F. Replenish the water bath using hot water if needed (note adding cold water can cause the jar to break). 
      • Turn off the crockpot and allow the jar to cool to room temperature in the water bath.
      • Strain the infused oil, composting or discarding the herbs.
      • Pour the oil into a sanitized, dark-colored 4 fl oz glass bottle.
      • Add the vitamin E oil and rosemary essential oil, and then fill the remainder of the bottle with the argan oil.
      • To use, rub 5-10 drops of herbal hair serum in your hands and then smooth throughout damp or dry hair (2-inches away from the root and down toward the end of the hair shaft) as desired.

Rosemary Holiday Tea Blend

Wrap yourself in the warmth and comfort of the holidays with a cup of this delightful tea. Notes of crisp winter mornings, with a dash of tart goodness, make this an easy and delicious recipe to share with loved ones. Did you know that rosemary can help support the digestive system? What a perfect herbal ally to have during the heaviness of holiday food gatherings. Pair rosemary with the bitterness of a citrus peel and the burst of flavor from cranberries, and your tastebuds and tummy will thank you. This can also be whipped up last minute as the perfect holiday gift with just a few simple ingredients, and you’ll have a lovely hostess gift or a short-notice gift. 

Ingredients

Handful of fresh rosemary (Salvia rosmarinus) leaf
2 pieces of fresh orange peel (remove the pith)
Handful of fresh cranberries

Directions

To enjoy right away:

      • Remove rosemary from the stem and place leaves in a small bowl. Peel and add orange peel to the bowl. Add a handful of fresh cranberries and mix together. Place tea mix into a pot and add 2 cups of water. Allow tea to simmer for 20 minutes, covered. Strain out the tea and pour into a mug. Add a spoonful of your favorite homemade herbal honey (this one is the cinnamon honey from the previous lesson). Stir and enjoy! 

To give as a gift:

      • Simply take a small glass jar and place the 3 ingredients inside, making sure that both the cranberries are at room temperature and the orange peel is free from any of the fruit. You’ll want to avoid any moisture in the jar. Wrap with a gift tag, and you’re all set!  Be sure to make this the day you are giving the gift, and let the recipient know to store it in the fridge if not making this tea blend right away. Blend will last up to 3 days before use.

Conclusion

While rosemary is often celebrated for its flavor and fragrance, this versatile herb offers so much more than solely culinary enjoyment. Its warming and stimulating properties not only enhance digestion but also support memory, circulation, and overall vitality. During the holidays, when traditions, family, and memories take center stage, rosemary provides both a symbolic and practical connection to well-being, making it a wonderful ally for both the mind and body. Whether used in tea, as part of a meal, or as a festive decoration, rosemary invites us to embrace its rich history and restorative qualities all season long.
